# Mission and scope

## Mission

Make documented animal family relationships and individual life histories easier to explore while preserving links to the source and the source's limits. The atlas is a public research aid, not an official studbook, a replacement for zoo records, or a complete census.

## Included v1 corpora

| Atlas | Included records | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Red panda | 83 named animals from the cited Futa family layer, with parent links, life events, and source records | Curated family subset only. A separate unlicensed 1,559-profile snapshot is excluded. |
| Polar bear | 22 named bears documented by Tallinn Zoo, Tierpark Berlin, and Prague Zoo | Public-source sample, not a European or international studbook. |
| Common hippopotamus | Bibi, Henry, Fiona, Tucker, and Fritz, with four supported parent links | Common hippopotamus (*Hippopotamus amphibius*) only; a small Cincinnati Zoo family. |

## Exclusions and open gaps

Unknown parents do not become synthetic animals. Unnamed births remain events. Animals are not connected based only on co-housing or expected breeding pairs. Unsupported translations, precise dates, destinations, and present-day living status remain absent or explicitly uncertain.

The polar-bear corpus omits records that appear only in restricted studbooks or unsupported secondary lists. The hippo atlas does not mix pygmy hippopotamuses with the common hippo dataset. All three corpora can grow when a public source identifies an individual and directly supports the proposed claim or event.

## Maintenance rule

Add a record only with an auditable source, exact source page, and preserved date precision. Recheck time-sensitive status and source conflicts before a new release. Official studbook custodians remain authoritative for their records.
