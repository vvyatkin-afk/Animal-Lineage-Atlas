Why this exists

A careful record of animal families

Animal Lineage Atlas brings individually named animals, sourced family relationships, and documented life events into one readable place. Each atlas states exactly which public records it covers and where the evidence ends.

Scope

The atlas and official studbooks

Official studbooks remain the authoritative records maintained by their custodians. This atlas complements them with a source-bounded public view; it does not replace a studbook or claim global completeness. Restricted or unlicensed records are not copied into this project.

How to read a record

Evidence and uncertainty

Parent links and other factual claims point to their evidence. Approximate dates stay approximate, unknown parents stay absent, and conflicting reports remain visible instead of being silently resolved.

Public use

Public animal photographs

The public site stores no copies of animal photographs. It embeds an external image only when recorded rights and terms permit it; otherwise the profile shows a neutral placeholder and a link to the original source page.

Future private use

A future personal offline archive

The shared media resolver is ready for a future private archive. An offline package can pair the app and data snapshot with a local media manifest while leaving the public atlas free of copied animal photos.

Manifest fields: original source URL, credit and rights metadata, relative local path, checksum, and archive status.

Keep the record useful

Corrections and contributions

Suggest a correction with the animal ID, the source URL, and the exact claim or event to review. New records should cite public primary evidence and preserve source date precision.

One canonical file per atlas

Data versions and review

Each atlas has one canonical data file. The hub computes record counts, relationship counts, source categories, data version, and review date from those files. Corrections keep the old evidence trail auditable.
